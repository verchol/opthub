# Investment decision methodology

Pre-registered. Written before any candidate output existed, and not to be
revised once results are seen — a rubric edited after the fact measures nothing.

## The decision

A partner at an early-stage fund reads one document and must choose:

| verdict | meaning |
|---|---|
| `INVEST` | would put the term sheet conversation on the calendar |
| `SECOND_MEETING` | interested, but needs a specific thing answered first |
| `PASS` | would not proceed |

The verdict is the outcome. Scores exist to make it auditable, not to replace it.

## Gates — any single failure caps the verdict at PASS

These are checked first and independently. A document that fails any of them
cannot be rescued by strength elsewhere, because each represents a claim a
partner would check and find false.

1. **No fabricated evidence.** Every named customer, number, competitor or
   citation is either traceable to the supplied source material or explicitly
   marked as an assumption. One invented fact fails the gate.
2. **The counter-case is present.** The document names the strongest argument
   against itself. A pitch that has none is a pitch that has not been stress
   tested.
3. **The ask is legible.** What is being raised, what it buys, and what it
   proves by the next milestone. "We're raising to grow" fails.

## Dimensions — scored 1-10 once the gates pass

| dimension | what earns a high score |
|---|---|
| `thesis` | The central claim is stated in one sentence and is falsifiable |
| `evidence` | Claims are carried by specific facts, not adjectives |
| `wedge` | It is clear why this team wins this specific beachhead now |
| `moat` | Explains what stops the obvious incumbent from absorbing it |
| `economics` | Unit economics are named, with the denominator that matters |
| `risk` | Names what would falsify the thesis, and what would be observed first |

## Thresholds

* `INVEST` — all gates pass, total ≥ 45/60, and no dimension below 6
* `SECOND_MEETING` — all gates pass, total ≥ 33/60
* `PASS` — otherwise

## The specific thesis under test

The document must argue that **AI optimization is multi-dimensional — spanning
routing, caching, context compaction, prompt and tool-schema shaping,
observability and attribution — and that because no single dimension is
sufficient, responsibility belongs with the workload owner through a central
solution rather than being scattered across individual teams.**

A document that argues this well but cannot survive the gates still fails. That
ordering is deliberate: an investor discounts a compelling argument to zero on
discovering one invented fact.
